Cooperative · Two players · One device

DUET

Fifteen agents hide in a grid of twenty-five codenames. You each see half the key — and neither half is the whole truth. Give one-word clues, find every agent, and don't touch an assassin.

Operatives

Names show up on the handoff screen so you always know whose turn it is.

Word pack

Every board is 25 words drawn at random from the pack you pick.

Difficulty

Difficulty is your turn budget. Fewer turns, tighter clues.

How to play

The idea

You're both on the same side. Twenty-five codenames sit on the table. Hidden among them are 15 agents you need to contact — but you can only see part of the map, and so can your partner.

Your key

  • Your key marks 9 agents and 3 assassins. Everything else is a bystander.
  • Your partner's key marks a different 9 and a different 3.
  • Only 3 agents appear on both keys, so 9 + 9 overlaps down to 15 agents total.
  • Here's the sting: a word that's a harmless bystander to you may be an assassin on your partner's key. You will never know which.

A turn

  • Say a one-word clue and a number — how many of your agents it points at. Type it in and hand the device over.
  • Your partner taps words. Keep guessing as long as you're right — there's no cap.
  • Tap a bystander and the turn ends. Tap an assassin and the mission is over.
  • You can stop guessing at any time. Stopping still costs a turn.

The clock

  • Every turn burns one token from your budget, whether it ended in a mistake or a voluntary stop.
  • Run out with agents still missing and you drop into sudden death: no more clues, just guesses from memory. One wrong tap and you lose.

Talking

  • Clue word plus number. That's it — no tone-of-voice hints, no "ooh, careful."
  • The clue can't be a word visible on the board, and it has to be about meaning, not spelling.
  • Compound words, proper nouns and names are fine. Play whatever you two agree on.

Reading the board

  • Cards outlined in green are your agents; the dark red ones are your assassins. You only see these while writing a clue.
  • A small dot in the corner during guessing is a quiet reminder of your own key — useful, since 3 of your agents are also your partner's.
  • Tan cards have been confirmed innocent. The dot on them shows whose key cleared it — a card cleared on one key can still be an agent on the other.
